01 / PROBLEM

What needed to work.

A single sentiment label loses detail. Reviews can praise aroma while criticizing taste, and missing ratings must not be silently treated as zero.

02 / APPROACH

The engineering behind it.

Share a text encoder across five regression targets, mask absent labels, split by product to prevent leakage, and save the adapter, prediction head and tokenizer as a recoverable serving bundle.

  1. 01

    Stable product-based splits and exact-text deduplication.

  2. 02

    Observed-label masks are applied to loss and metrics.

  3. 03

    LoRA query/value adapters keep a separately trainable regression head.

  4. 04

    Reloaded predictions are checked against the saved model output.

TECHNICAL DEEP DIVE

From implementation to evidence.

Prevent leakage and distinguish missing ratings

Product-held-out partitions and duplicate-text checks reduce leakage between train and test. Missing targets are masked in the regression loss and metrics rather than treated as real zero ratings. Five normalized outputs represent appearance, aroma, palate, taste and overall; they are regression estimates, not confidence probabilities.

Verify the serving artifact

The LoRA smoke workflow ran 13 tests and one CPU training epoch, then saved a serving bundle and verified reload and prediction. The separate larger baseline evaluation used a 20,000-record prefix, 3,559 test reviews and 76 held-out products: mean predictor macro MSE 0.018400 versus Ridge 0.016149, a 12.2% reduction on that evaluation. Different partitions prevent attributing that reduction to LoRA.

04 / SCOPE & TRADEOFFS

Know what the result means.

LoRA and ridge use different source prefixes and partitions. Their MSE values do not support a direct ranking; the 46-review LoRA test set is a workflow check.
